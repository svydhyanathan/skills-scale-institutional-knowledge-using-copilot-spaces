# OctoAcme Project Management Documentation

## OctoAcme Project Management Processes

OctoAcme runs projects through a structured lifecycle: initiation, planning, execution, release, and close/retrospective. Initiation confirms the business need, measurable outcomes, stakeholders, timeline, resources, and go/no-go decision through a project one-pager. Planning turns an approved initiative into prioritized, estimated work with clear acceptance criteria, dependencies, risks, milestones, a release plan, and a Definition of Done.

Execution uses an iterative delivery rhythm of project-board tracking, daily standups, weekly delivery syncs, demos, and end-of-sprint reviews. Work moves from backlog through ready, in progress, review, QA, and done. Small pull requests, issue links, automated CI and security checks, test coverage, manual QA where needed, and peer approval keep changes controlled and reviewable. Risks and blockers are monitored in the risk register and escalated through team, PM, Product Lead or dependent teams, and sponsor levels as needed.

The process has clear ownership across roles: the Project Manager coordinates delivery and communications, the Product Manager owns outcomes and prioritization, developers implement and test solutions, QA validates quality and acceptance, and stakeholders provide input and approvals. Communication uses a regular cadence of PM–Product alignment, delivery standups, stakeholder updates, weekly risk reviews, and a shared source of truth. Reports cover progress, next steps, risks, blockers, and decisions needed.

Release readiness requires acceptance criteria to be met, passing CI and security scans, prepared smoke tests, release notes, and rollback or mitigation plans. Teams deploy through staging and production verification, announce releases, and respond to incidents using on-call and escalation procedures. At close, retrospectives capture lessons and convert them into prioritized, assigned improvement actions with due dates and success criteria, supported by the principles of customer-first value, iterative delivery, clear ownership, data-informed decisions, and psychological safety.
